# Sales Funnel Guide

# Signal vs Noise for 1Putt Health

## Your Current Reality Check

**You’re selling professional services** - this is fundamentally different from SaaS. Your funnel won’t look like a typical startup’s metrics dashboard.

---

## What is SIGNAL (Pay Attention to This)

### Lead Quality Indicators

✅ **Inbound from content** - Someone found your “New to Healthcare” page and booked a call
✅ **Specific technical questions** - “We need help with Epic CDS Hooks implementation”
✅ **Budget mentioned early** - “We have $150K allocated for this project”
✅ **Timeline urgency** - “We need to launch in Q2 to hit our Series A milestones”
✅ **Technical decision-maker on call** - CTO/VP Eng, not just a PM or recruiter
✅ **Referrals from past clients** - Highest conversion rate, shortest sales cycle
✅ **Funded startups** (Series A+) or established companies with revenue

### Deal Progression Signals

✅ **They send you technical specs unprompted** - Shows seriousness
✅ **They want to introduce you to their team** - Buying signal
✅ **They ask about your availability/capacity** - Thinking about timeline
✅ **They share their technical architecture** - Trust building
✅ **Follow-up within 24-48 hours** - Engaged buyer
✅ **Multiple stakeholders join calls** - Decision-making process moving

### Business Health Signals

✅ **Repeat customers** - Your best growth indicator
✅ **Project extensions** - “Can you stay on another 3 months?”
✅ **Scope expansion** - “Can you also help with HIPAA compliance?”
✅ **Referrals per project** - Aim for 1-2 qualified referrals per completed project

---

## What is NOISE (Don’t Optimize for This)

### Vanity Metrics

❌ **Website traffic** - Unless it converts to qualified leads
❌ **Newsletter subscribers** - Nice to have, not a business metric
❌ **Social media followers** - Doesn’t pay bills
❌ **Content engagement** - Views/likes mean nothing without conversions
❌ **Inbound volume from recruiters** - They want to place their people, not hire you

### Time Wasters

❌ **“Pick your brain” coffee chats** - No budget, just fishing for free advice
❌ **Students/recent grads** - Not your customer (unless they’re funded founders)
❌ **Enterprise RFPs with 40 vendors** - Low win rate, high effort
❌ **Unclear project scope** - “We’re exploring options” = not ready to buy
❌ **“Advisor” requests** - Usually means free work for equity
❌ **Conversations without budget/timeline** - Qualify hard, disqualify fast

### False Urgency

❌ **“We need a proposal by Friday”** (but no decision timeline) - Tire kickers
❌ **Multiple “final” rounds** - Procurement theater
❌ **Asking for detailed spec work before contract** - Free consulting trap

## Your Actual Funnel (Services Business)

```
AWARENESS → QUALIFICATION → PROPOSAL → NEGOTIATION → CONTRACT → DELIVERY → EXPANSION/REFERRAL
```

### Stage 1: Awareness

**Sources of leads:**
- Inbound (content, SEO, word of mouth)
- Outbound (targeted outreach to funded startups)
- Referrals (your best source)
- Speaking/content marketing

**Metrics that matter:**
- Qualified inbound leads per month (not total traffic)
- Referrals per completed project

### Stage 2: Qualification (CRITICAL - This is where you win against FortyAU)

**Your qualification criteria - Healthcare-Specific:**

**Must-haves:**
- Healthcare/digital health company
- Has budget ($50K+ project minimum)
- Has timeline (launching in 6 months or less)
- Technical decision-maker involved (CTO, VP Eng, not just PM)
- Specific EHR integration problem (Epic, Cerner, etc.)

**UNIQUE QUALIFIERS (Your competitive advantage):**
- ✅ **Bespoke integration complexity** - Site-specific Epic instances, custom CDS Hooks, multi-instance deployments
- ✅ **EHR vendor fragmentation** - Targeting 2-3 EHR vendors representing 80%+ of their market
- ✅ **Middleware evaluation done** - They’ve already talked to Redox/Particle and discovered the economics don’t work
- ✅ **Failed first attempt** - Previous agency couldn’t handle Epic API quirks or certification process
- ✅ **Analytics use case** - Where middleware PMPM costs destroy unit economics at scale

**Red flags (disqualify fast - send to FortyAU):**
- ❌ “What’s your hourly rate?” (value-based pricing is better)
- ❌ Simple SMART on FHIR read-only app (commodity work, FortyAU can handle)
- ❌ No specific EHR vendor identified yet
- ❌ “We’re just exploring” (not ready to buy)
- ❌ Only want offshore rates
- ❌ Single-site, standard FHIR integration (not complex enough for your expertise)

**Healthcare-Specific Screening Questions (Ask before discovery call):**
1. **Which EHR vendors are you integrating with?** (Epic, Cerner, other?)
2. **How many different Epic instances will you deploy to?** (1 site vs 50 sites)
3. **Have you evaluated middleware platforms like Redox or Particle?** (What did you learn?)
4. **What’s your use case?** (Transactional workflow vs analytics aggregation)
5. **What’s your patient/member volume at scale?** (Helps assess middleware PMPM impact)
6. **Have you attempted this integration before?** (Failed attempts = qualified lead)

**Time investment:** 30-min discovery call max. If they’re not qualified, politely pass and refer to FortyAU or middleware vendor.

### Stage 3: Proposal

**Signal:** They asked for it after a good discovery call
**Noise:** They asked for a proposal in their first email (not qualified yet)

**Your proposal should:**
- Be tied to business outcomes, not hours
- Include 3 options (good, better, best)
- Have clear scope boundaries
- Include timeline and milestones
- Be delivered within 3-5 business days max

**Conversion benchmark:** 30-50% of proposals should close if you’re qualifying well

### Stage 4: Negotiation

**Signal:** They’re negotiating scope/price, not asking for 50% off
**Noise:** “Can you do this for half the price?”

**What to track:**
- Time from proposal to signed contract (aim for <2 weeks)
- Common objections (helps you refine positioning)

### Stage 5: Contract → Delivery

**Not part of sales funnel, but critical for the next stage**

### Stage 6: Expansion/Referral

**Your real business growth engine:**
- Can you extend the engagement?
- Can you expand scope?
- Can they refer you to 1-2 similar companies?

## Your Blind Spots (Common for Technical Founders)

### 1. Not Qualifying Hard Enough

You’re too nice. You take calls with anyone. Stop.

**Fix:** Have a qualification form on your contact page. Require:
- Company name
- Funding status / revenue
- Project timeline
- Estimated budget range
- Specific problem description

### 2. Underpricing

You’re thinking hourly rates. You should think value-based pricing.

**Example:**
- ❌ “We charge $200/hour for development”
- ✅ “Epic CDS Hooks integration that prevents medication errors: $120K fixed-price project”

### 3. No Clear Disqualification Criteria

You should say “no” to 70%+ of inbound leads.

**Auto-disqualify:**
- Pre-seed with no funding
- “We want to hire you full-time” (unless you want that)
- Projects under $25K (unless strategic)
- No clear decision-maker
- No budget/timeline

### 4. Not Asking for Referrals

At project completion, explicitly ask: “Who else do you know building in healthcare who might need help with [X]?”

### 5. Spending Too Much Time on Content/Marketing

Your “New to Healthcare” page is great. Don’t spend 6 months writing 50 blog posts.

**Better use of time:**
- Reach out to 10 Series A healthcare startups per week
- Ask past clients for intros
- Speak at 1-2 healthcare tech conferences per year

### 6. Not Having a Standard Process

Every deal feels custom. That’s inefficient.

**Create:**
- Standard discovery call agenda
- Standard proposal template (3 tiers)
- Standard project kickoff process
- Standard offboarding/referral ask process

### 7. Thinking Like a Startup (Metrics-Obsessed)

You don’t need 47 metrics. You need 5:
1. Qualified leads per month
2. Proposals sent
3. Proposals won (close rate %)
4. Average deal size
5. Referrals per project

## Discovery Call Script Template

### Pre-Call Prep (5 min)

- Review qualification form
- Check LinkedIn for company/person
- Look up funding status (Crunchbase)
- Review their website/product

### Call Structure (30 min) - Healthcare-Specific

**1. Introduction (5 min)**
- Quick intro of you and 1Putt Health
- Confirm time limit (30 min)
- Set agenda: understand their needs, see if we’re a fit
- **Positioning statement:** “We specialize in bespoke healthcare integrations where cookie-cutter approaches fail—things like site-specific Epic instances, custom CDS Hooks, and analytics use cases where middleware economics break down.”

**2. Their Situation (15 min) - COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE QUESTIONS**
Ask open-ended questions:
- “Tell me about what you’re building”
- “What’s the specific challenge you’re facing with [Epic/Cerner] integration?”
- **“Which EHR vendors are you targeting? How many different instances?”** (Complexity signal)
- **“Have you talked to middleware platforms like Redox or Particle? What did you learn?”** (Economic awareness)
- **“Have you talked to other agencies or consultancies about this?”** (Competitive intelligence - are they talking to FortyAU?)
- **“What have you tried so far?”** (Failed attempts = qualified lead)
- **“What’s your use case - transactional workflow or analytics aggregation?”** (Middleware vs custom decision)
- **“What’s your expected patient/member volume at scale?”** (Assess middleware PMPM impact)
- “What happens if you don’t solve this?” (Business impact)
- “When do you need this solved by?” (Timeline)
- “What’s your budget range for this project?” (Budget)
- “Who else is involved in this decision?” (Decision-making process)

**LISTEN FOR THESE COMPETITIVE SIGNALS:**
- ✅ “The other firm suggested middleware, but the costs scale poorly” → You win
- ✅ “We tried a standard SMART app but each Epic instance behaves differently” → You win
- ✅ “We need CDS Hooks customized for different health systems” → You win
- ❌ “We just need a basic read-only SMART app” → Refer to FortyAU
- ❌ “We’re building for a single Epic instance” → Probably not complex enough

**3. Your Solution (5 min) - DIFFERENTIATION**
- Brief overview of how you’ve solved similar problems
- **Mention specific EHR vendor quirks you’ve navigated** (Epic API behavior, certification process)
- **Reference economic modeling:** “For example, with [past client], middleware would have cost them $X PMPM which didn’t work at their scale. We built a custom integration for $Y that paid for itself in Z months.”
- Mention relevant case studies (Lexmed, Practice Rounds)
- Don’t go too deep - save for proposal

**4. Competitive Positioning (if they mentioned other firms)**
- **If they mentioned FortyAU or similar:** “They’re a great firm for general software development. Where we’re different is the site-specific EHR expertise—we’ve deployed to X different Epic instances and know that what works in the sandbox doesn’t always work in production.”
- **If they mentioned middleware:** “Middleware platforms are excellent for transactional workflows and fragmented markets. For your use case [analytics/multi-instance/etc], the economics favor a custom approach because [specific reason].”

**5. Next Steps (5 min)**
- If qualified: “I’d like to put together a proposal. I’ll send it by [date].”
- If not qualified but good fit for competitor: “Based on what you’ve shared, I think [FortyAU/middleware vendor] might be a better fit because [reason]. Happy to make an intro.”
- If not qualified at all: “Based on what you’ve shared, I don’t think we’re the right fit because [reason]. Have you considered [alternative]?”
- Set clear expectations for follow-up

### Post-Call (15 min)

- Update Notion immediately
- Mark as Qualified or Disqualified
- If qualified, schedule proposal delivery
- Send calendar invite for follow-up call

### Economic Comparison (Healthcare-Specific - NEW SECTION)

**Why this matters:** Differentiate from middleware platforms and demonstrate ROI

**Include comparison table:**

| Approach | Upfront Cost | Ongoing Cost (Annual) | 3-Year Total | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Middleware Platform** (Redox/Particle) | $20-50K integration | $1-3 PMPM × volume | $X (scales with volume) | Transactional workflows, fragmented EHR market |
| **Custom Integration** (1Putt Health) | $120-200K | $15-30K maintenance | $Y (fixed cost) | Analytics use cases, 2-3 EHR vendors, high volume |

**Example calculation for their use case:**
- At 50,000 patients, middleware costs $X/year
- At 500,000 patients, middleware costs $Y/year
- Custom integration pays for itself at Z patients (typically 12-18 months for analytics use cases)

**Additional advantages of custom approach:**
- Site-specific customization for Epic instance variations
- No per-patient recurring fees
- Full control over integration logic
- Faster performance (direct API calls, no middleware latency)
- Better suited for CDS Hooks and real-time workflows

### Three Options (1 page each)

**Option 1: Essential** ($X)
- Core deliverables only
- Timeline: X weeks
- What’s included
- What’s not included

**Option 2: Recommended** ($X + 30-50%)
- Everything in Essential plus…
- Additional scope items
- Timeline: X weeks
- This should be the one you want them to pick

**Option 3: Premium** ($X + 80-100%)
- Everything in Recommended plus…
- Extended scope, faster timeline, or ongoing support
- Timeline: X weeks

### Timeline & Milestones

- Week-by-week breakdown
- Key deliverables
- Decision points

### Investment & Terms

- Payment schedule (typically 50% upfront, 50% at completion)
- Payment methods
- Proposal validity (14 days)
- Terms & conditions

### Next Steps

- Accept proposal
- Schedule kickoff
- Contract signing

### Appendix

- Team bios
- Case studies

## Competitive Differentiation Strategy

### When You Win vs FortyAU

**You win when:**
- ✅ Multi-instance Epic deployments (10+ health systems)
- ✅ Site-specific EHR customization required
- ✅ CDS Hooks with complex clinical logic
- ✅ Analytics use case where middleware PMPM destroys economics
- ✅ Previous integration attempt failed
- ✅ Patient volume >100K (custom integration ROI is clear)
- ✅ Client values deep EHR vendor expertise over general software development

**FortyAU wins when:**
- ❌ Simple SMART on FHIR read-only app
- ❌ Single Epic instance
- ❌ Client prioritizes “top 1% talent” brand over healthcare specialization
- ❌ General software development with light healthcare component
- ❌ They don’t understand the difference between cookie-cutter and bespoke

### When You Win vs Middleware Vendors

**You win when:**
- ✅ Analytics/aggregation use case (not transactional)
- ✅ Patient volume >50K (middleware costs scale poorly)
- ✅ Targeting 2-3 EHR vendors (80%+ market coverage)
- ✅ Need custom CDS Hooks or real-time workflows
- ✅ Performance/latency matters (direct API > middleware proxy)
- ✅ Client has technical team that can maintain integration

**Middleware wins when:**
- ❌ Fragmented EHR market (10+ vendors)
- ❌ Low patient volume (<10K)
- ❌ Transactional workflows (ADT, lab results, etc.)
- ❌ Client has no technical team
- ❌ Rapid time-to-market is only priority

### Positioning Scripts

**Against FortyAU:**
> “FortyAU is an excellent firm for general software development. Where we’re different is the depth of EHR vendor expertise—we’ve deployed to 50+ different Epic instances and know that what works in the sandbox doesn’t always work in production. Each instance has different configurations, workflows, and quirks. That’s where our specialized healthcare integration experience creates value.”

**Against Middleware:**
> “Middleware platforms like Redox are excellent for transactional workflows and fragmented EHR markets. But for your use case—analytics aggregation across 100K+ patients—the economics favor a custom approach. At your scale, middleware would cost you $1-3 PMPM which is $1.2-3.6M annually. We can build a custom integration for $150-200K that you own outright with $20-30K annual maintenance.”

**Against “We’re building in-house”:**
> “That makes sense for some companies. The question is whether EHR integration is core to your IP or a commodity enabler. Most digital health companies find that building and maintaining Epic integrations across multiple instances becomes a significant ongoing cost—every Epic version upgrade, every App Orchard recertification, every site-specific configuration. We let you focus on your differentiated IP while we handle the undifferentiated heavy lifting.”

### Win/Loss Analysis (Track in Notion)

After every deal (won or lost), ask:
1. **What competitors did they evaluate?**
2. **Why did they choose us?** (or why did we lose?)
3. **What was the deciding factor?** (Price, expertise, timeline, fit?)
4. **What could we have done differently?**

**Pattern to watch for:**
- If you’re losing to FortyAU on “general development” projects → You’re not qualifying hard enough for complexity
- If you’re losing to middleware on “cost” → You’re not modeling the economics clearly enough
- If you’re winning against both → Document what made that deal your sweet spot
