# How Insurance Discovery Works

Insurance discovery is an automated process designed to uncover active insurance coverage that patients did not present or were unaware of at the time of service. Unlike standard eligibility verification, which validates known policies, discovery proactively searches for previously unknown or undisclosed coverage, thus reducing the volume of self-pay encounters.

The discovery process integrates multiple sophisticated data sources:

- **Clearinghouse Networks:** Nationwide eligibility and claims transaction data monitoring to detect recent insurance activity.

- **Direct Payer Connections:** Real-time queries via APIs and payer portals to national and regional insurance databases.

- **Government Sources:** Integration with state Medicaid enrollment systems and specialized databases, such as Medicare Beneficiary ID (MBI) [lookup](https://www.cms.gov/training-education/partner-outreach-resources/new-medicare-card/getting-mbis) services.

- **Credit & Consumer Data:** Utilizing credit header files and demographic information to enhance matching accuracy across disparate records.

- **Specialized Third-Party Liability (TPL) Sources:** Unique databases for accident-related claims, including [ISO ClaimSearch](https://www.verisk.com/products/claimsearch/), state DMV crash reports, and insurance adjuster portals.

These platforms employ probabilistic matching algorithms and predictive analytics to connect patient identities across multiple systems despite spelling variations, outdated information, or incomplete data. Coverage discovery can occur:

- Pre-service (during patient scheduling)

- Point-of-service (common in emergency or trauma settings)

- Post-service (typically within 72 hours to 30 days post-discharge)

- Continuous monitoring through timely-filing deadlines

When valid coverage is discovered, systems automatically update the hospital's EHR and trigger eligibility checks to confirm coverage details.

# Market Landscape

The insurance discovery market segments into three primary categories, largely defined by integration depth and breadth of data access:

- **Clearinghouse and End-to-End RCM Suites:** Vendors such as [Experian](https://elion.health/products/experian-coverage-discovery), [Inovalon](https://elion.health/products/inovalon-insurance-discovery), [Optum](https://elion.health/products/optum-coverage-insights), [Office Ally](https://elion.health/products/office-ally-revenue-recovery), and [Waystar](https://elion.health/products/waystar-coverage-detection) dominate this segment. Their large-scale clearinghouse operations provide deep data visibility across commercial and governmental insurance transactions, facilitating integration into broader revenue-cycle platforms.

- **Front-End RCM Platforms:** Vendors including [FinThrive](https://elion.health/products/finthrive-insurance-discovery), [Infinx](https://elion.health/products/infinx-patient-access-plus), and [ZOLL Data AR Boost](https://elion.health/products/zoll-ar-boost) focus primarily on front-end RCM functions such as eligibility verification, prior authorization, and patient financial estimation. Insurance discovery complements these core capabilities, enabling rapid deployment across diverse provider environments.

- **Targeted and Specialty Solutions:** Providers like [MaxRTE](https://elion.health/products/maxrte), [eInsights](https://elion.health/products/einsights), [Nirvana](https://elion.health/products/nirvana), and [TevixMD](https://elion.health/products/tevixmd) deliver niche-focused SaaS or API-driven solutions optimized for specific healthcare segments (e.g. behavioral health) or speed of implementation, generally under subscription rather than contingency pricing models.

# Future Outlook

Recent policy shifts, data access advancements, and AI-driven innovations are reshaping the insurance discovery landscape:

- **Policy Changes:** The 2025 federal budget reconciliation (OBBBA) [reduces](https://www.sellersdorsey.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Congressional-Approved-Reconciliation-Bill_070325_FINAL.pdf) Medicaid's retroactive eligibility window from 90 to 60 days for traditional beneficiaries, tightening hospitals' window to identify coverage.

- **Data Liberalization:** CMS's [Provider-Access API](https://fire.ly/blog/understanding-the-provider-access-api/) mandate requires payers to offer real-time insurance data via standardized FHIR endpoints by 2027, significantly enhancing discovery accuracy.

- **AI Investment Surge:** Industry leaders are aggressively investing in artificial intelligence to enhance predictive matching capabilities. Noteworthy examples include [Waystar's](https://elion.health/products/waystar-coverage-detection) recent $12.5 million secondary [offering](https://www.waystar.com/news/waystar-announces-proposed-public-offering-of-common-stock-2/) aimed at developing next-generation discovery APIs and [FinThrive's](https://elion.health/products/finthrive-insurance-discovery) [launch](https://finthrive.com/news/finthrive-introduces-agentic-ai-at-hfma-2025-to-help-customers-transform-healthcare-revenue-cycle-management-performance) of Agentic AI capabilities, designed to autonomously learn payer behaviors and accelerate revenue recognition.

Hospitals evaluating insurance discovery solutions must balance comprehensive platform integration, targeted efficiency, and niche capabilities to optimize revenue capture and mitigate financial risk in an increasingly demanding healthcare landscape.
