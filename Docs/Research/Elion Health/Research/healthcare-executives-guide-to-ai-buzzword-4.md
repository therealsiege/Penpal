So, how do agents and agentic AI fit into this progression? Think of agents as the next evolutionary step where LLMs graduate from passive text generators to active problem solvers. Unlike traditional chatbots that simply respond to prompts, agentic AI systems can break down complex tasks, make decisions, use tools, and execute multi-step workflows autonomously. Agents combine the language understanding of LLMs with planning capabilities, memory, and the ability to interact with external systems, creating AI that doesn't just generate content but takes action.

# **The Core Components of AI Agents**

At their heart, AI agents are autonomous software systems built around three key capabilities:

1. **Reasoning Engine:** Usually powered by an LLM, this "brain" interprets instructions, plans solutions, and orchestrates the other components. The LLMs powering most agents are multimodal allowing input to come via text, image or sound.

2. **Memory Systems:** Both short-term memory (for maintaining immediate context) and long-term memory (for retaining knowledge and learning from experience) enable agents to operate consistently and improve over time. In most situations, each time an agent tackles a problem the agent is aware of what happened during the last attempt.

3. **Tool Integration:** External APIs, databases, and programs serve as the agent's "hands," allowing it to take real actions like scheduling appointments, querying records, or updating systems. Agents can interact with APIs, search or browse the web, and even login with a username and password to protected websites.

Agents represent a meaningful step forward when compared to historical automation techniques. While robotic process automation (RPA) and other automation tools excel at high-volume, repetitive tasks with clear rules, they tend to be brittle and struggle with variability and ambiguity.

Both agents and automation tend to approach a problem with a goal in mind, but they differ significantly in their approach. Most automation systems are highly deterministic and tend operate through navigating pre-determined decision trees: "If A happens, do B." Agents, on the other hand, are given a problem, context, and goal, but have “autonomy” in deciding how to deconstruct the problem and what tools to use.

This ability to handle ambiguity is one of the key advancements that make agents so appealing. There’s no need for endless if-this-then-that statements handling a laundry list of edge cases or hoping a portal website doesn’t change designs because the workflow breaks.

# **Where are agents making an impact in healthcare?**

AI agents are already transforming several critical areas of healthcare operations. In [RCM](https://elion.health/categories/revenue-cycle-management-rcm), agents help prevent denials by proactively scanning for issues and gathering documentation. When denials do occur, agents can investigate root causes, classify denial reasons, and route claims to appropriate work queues with supporting documentation attached without human intervention.

Another area where agents are already making a significant impact is voice AI. Unlike simple interactive voice response (IVR) systems, voice agents can maintain complex conversations, understanding context and nuance. In healthcare revenue cycle management, for example, voice agents now handle calls with payers to check claim status, verify benefits, or initiate prior authorizations—tasks that historically consumed hours of staff time.

On the clinical side, agents assist with tasks requiring rapid analysis and contextual understanding. They [organize treatment plans](https://elion.health/products/lumeon), [guide care decisions](https://elion.health/products/regard), and [help match patients to clinical trials](https://elion.health/products/tempus-oncology).  Agents have demonstrated success in performing basic clinical tasks within virtual EHR environments, suggesting they may handle more routine use-cases at scale soon.

The next frontier will likely involve multi-agent systems, where specialized agents collaborate to handle even more complex workflows. The future isn't about replacing humans but about creating powerful tools that augment their capabilities, allowing them to focus on the most impactful aspects of their work.
