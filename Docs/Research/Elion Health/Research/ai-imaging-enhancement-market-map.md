For decades, hospitals believed the only solution to aging, slow imaging fleets was massive capital expenditure. Today, thanks to AI, it’s possible to imagine transforming a twenty-year-old MRI scanner into a state-of-the-art machine without spending millions on new hardware.

Why does this matter? Radiologic technologist vacancy rates have [tripled](https://www.rsna.org/news/2024/october/radiologic-technologist-shortage) from 6% in 2019 to approximately 18% in 2024, creating severe operational bottlenecks. MRI scans on legacy machines [take](https://www.healthline.com/health/how-long-does-an-mri-take) up to 45 minutes or more (depending on type of scan), but newer, faster machines [cost](https://www.excedr.com/blog/how-much-does-an-mri-machine-cost) upward of $1 million. This creates a compounding crisis: fewer technologists trying to operate slow equipment while patient backlogs grow.

[AI image enhancement](https://elion.health/categories/ai-imaging-enhancement/products) offers a fundamentally different approach. Rather than replacing hardware, it uses deep learning to reconstruct diagnostic-quality images from faster, lower-dose scans. The result is a "virtual upgrade," extending the lifespan of existing equipment, doubling patient throughput, and avoiding multi-million-dollar capital outlays.

# What image enhancement software does (and doesn't do)

The [AI imaging enhancement](https://elion.health/categories/ai-imaging-enhancement/products) category comprises software that optimizes the raw pixels of a medical image, not the diagnosis itself. The distinction matters. These tools don't detect pathology or generate reports. Instead, they use machine learning techniques like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs) to improve image quality at or near the point of acquisition.

The core functions include noise reduction (cleaning up grainy images from low-dose or rapid scans), resolution enhancement (sharpening anatomical details), artifact correction (removing distortions from patient motion or metal implants), and accelerated reconstruction (generating high-quality images from under-sampled data).

This category is distinct from [workflow and diagnostic AI tools](https://elion.health/categories/ai-imaging-clinical-decision-support/products) that flag potential pathology, imaging IT infrastructure like PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication Systems), and reporting software. Image enhancement tools output an improved DICOM image series, not a clinical finding. They sit between the scanner and storage system, processing images automatically before radiologists see them.

# Market drivers reshaping adoption

Four converging pressures are accelerating deployment of these solutions across health systems.

1. **The workforce crisis is acute.** With technologist vacancies at [18%](https://www.rsna.org/news/2024/october/radiologic-technologist-shortage), imaging departments need to scan more patients with fewer staff. AI enhancement enables this by cutting scan times up to 80% and reducing repeat exams caused by motion artifacts or poor image quality. One [hospital](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30292264/) implementing faster MRI protocols reduced pediatric anesthesia use from 57% of cases to 5% for children ages 4-6, demonstrating both safety and efficiency gains.

2. **Regulatory pressure is creating financial consequences for inaction.** Medicare's [XR-29 "Smart Dose" standard](https://www.medicalimaging.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Is-Your-CT-Smart-Dose-Compliant-Whitepaper.pdf) imposes a 15% payment reduction on noncompliant CT scanners (i.e. scanners that don’t meet all of the four requirements: DICOM Radiation Dose Structured Reporting, Dose Check Features, Automatic Exposure Control or Reference Protocols). This regulation, in effect since 2016, [legitimized third-party software](https://www.oncologysystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/7253.pdf) as a compliance solution and forced the market for dose reduction tools into existence. Health systems can now meet ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) principles through software rather than hardware replacement.

3. **The financial case is straightforward.** CFOs can defer multi-million-dollar scanner purchases by deploying AI software that costs a fraction of new equipment. Vendors like AIRS Medical specifically market compatibility with legacy scanners, allowing health systems to extract additional years from aging fleets while maintaining modern image quality standards.

4. **Patient experience improvements are measurable.** Reducing MRI scan times from 45 minutes to 10-15 minutes dramatically improves comfort and compliance. For CT and PET imaging, AI denoising allows technicians to use radiation doses up to 75% lower while maintaining diagnostic quality. These aren't incremental gains. They represent fundamental improvements in safety and patient tolerance.

# AI imaging enhancement vendor landscape and strategic positioning

The market includes both hardware manufacturer-developed solutions as well as vendor-neutral options, each with different implications for health system integration and ROI.

## **OEM-integrated reconstruction engines**

Scanner manufacturers embed AI directly into new equipment or offer licensed software upgrades. [GE HealthCare's AIR Recon DL](https://elion.health/products/air-recon-dl) and [TrueFidelity](https://elion.health/products/ge-healthcare-truefidelity-ct), [Siemens Healthineers' Deep Resolve](https://elion.health/products/siemens-deep-resolve), and [Philips' SmartSpeed Precise](https://elion.health/products/philips-smartspeed-precise) represent this category. These solutions offer seamless workflow integration and are ideal for health systems standardizing on a single hardware vendor. The tradeoff is vendor lock-in and limited applicability to mixed fleets.

## **Vendor-neutral MRI and PET platforms**

Third-party software designed for compatibility across manufacturers offers maximum flexibility for providers with diverse equipment. [Subtle Medical](https://elion.health/products/subtle-medical)'s SubtleMR and SubtlePET enable scan time reductions of 60-80%, deployable via cloud or on-premise servers. [AIRS Medical's SwiftMR](https://elion.health/products/airsmed-swiftmr) specifically targets legacy scanner compatibility, breathing new life into machines that are 25-30 years old. These platforms process DICOM images post-acquisition, operating independently of scanner manufacturer.

## **Vendor-neutral CT and multi-modality solutions**

[ClariPi's](https://elion.health/products/clarict-ai) [ClariCT.AI](http://clarict.ai/) focuses on ultra-low-dose CT enhancement to reduce noise while preserving diagnostic detail. [Claritas HealthTech's iRAD](https://elion.health/products/claritas-irad) takes a broader approach, supporting multiple modalities including digital radiography, CT, MRI, and ultrasound. This multi-modality capability positions these vendors for health systems seeking standardized image quality across their entire imaging service line.

## **Specialized applications**

Some vendors apply image enhancement to enable entirely new clinical workflows. [Spectronic Medical](https://elion.health/products/spectronic-medical)’s MRI Planner generates synthetic CT images from MRI data for radiotherapy planning, eliminating the need for separate CT scans in radiation oncology. [MediView](https://elion.health/products/mediview-xr)'s XR90 uses augmented reality to project 3D holographic anatomy derived from CT and ultrasound, guiding needle-based procedures with greater precision.

# **What's next for image enhancement**

The technology is moving beyond post-processing toward real-time optimization. Future systems will provide dynamic feedback to scanners, adjusting acquisition parameters on the fly or terminating scans once sufficient data is captured. Patient-specific tailoring will allow algorithms to adapt enhancement levels based on individual anatomy and clinical questions.

As image quality standardizes, downstream automation becomes more reliable. Diagnostic AI tools perform better on consistently enhanced images, enabling more accurate automated measurements and opportunistic screening for conditions like osteoporosis or coronary calcium. DICOM working groups are developing formal standards for identifying and validating AI-reconstructed images, which will accelerate adoption once established.

The trajectory is clear: AI image enhancement is shifting from optional optimization to foundational infrastructure. Health systems that implement these solutions now are extending asset lifespans, increasing patient access through higher throughput, and creating more sustainable departmental economics.
