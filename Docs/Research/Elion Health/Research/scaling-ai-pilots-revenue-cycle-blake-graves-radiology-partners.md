**Name:** Blake Graves

**Role:** SVP of Revenue Cycle Management

**Org:** Radiology Partners

**To start off, can you share a bit about your background and Radiology Partners’ business model?** Radiology Partners is the largest radiology practice in the world, with over 4,000 radiologists serving hospital sites and imaging centers across all 50 states. I’m the Senior Vice President of Revenue Cycle Management, patient experience, and I oversee critical business projects around efficiency and real estate.

**What are your top technology priorities today?** Technology is central to everything we do in the revenue cycle. We operate on Imagine Software’s revenue management system, which includes as part of the core tech stack a great deal of automation including AI solutions. Beyond that, we’re looking at ways to optimize our revenue cycle through technology with two key priorities: increasing the efficiency of our business and using AI and technology to drive revenue yield.

**We’ve spoken before about your pilot-first approach to evaluating AI. What does that look like in practice?** We made the decision at the beginning of this AI expansion in the market that we needed to be willing to fail fast. We run short, structured pilots, typically 120 days to six months, across a wide range of AI applications: autonomous coding, eligibility, and denials management. We move quickly to determine whether there’s ROI, and we make implementation decisions quickly.  If it’s working, we scale. If not, we move on.

**Doesn’t that pilot approach still require significant time and cost to integrate up front?** I think it's a real consideration. The biggest thing we assess is the capacity strain or compression that it puts on our teammates relative to what it costs and the value it can deliver. Most vendors are eager to get started and will support a fast stand-up because they believe their product will show value.

Additionally, we’ve built a dedicated internal team to manage AI discovery and pilot support, which helps us move fast without pulling focus from our day-to-day operations.

**You’ve spent a lot of time evaluating solutions in the autonomous coding space. Can you share your experience and the solution you eventually settled on?**

In our experience, the top vendors ultimately do what they say they’ll do. We saw autonomous rates in the 60-80% range for diagnostic radiology for the vendors we worked with. But we ultimately landed on a more cost-effective solution: a combination of Imagine’s embedded automation, augmented by our offshore partner with their own automation layer. That gives us similar performance at about one-third of the cost.

**That’s a pretty significant difference.** It is. And it’s not just in coding. We saw the same thing on the patient payments side. The top solutions work well, but we found more mid-tier solutions that deliver the same results at a fraction of the cost. I think more sophisticated provider orgs are starting to realize you can get the same horsepower without the big price tag, especially in areas where the underlying tech isn’t rocket science.

**Do you build internally at all?** Yes. We’ve built internal bots to support No Surprises Act dispute resolution and we’re also experimenting with bots for eligibility and denial work. It’s still early, but we want to see if we can match vendor performance with internal resources, especially for simpler tasks.

**How do you think about the build vs. buy decision?** We often pilot both side-by-side, and we’re evaluating both speed and value. Our teams aren’t software developers by trade, but if we're able to do things quickly that other people are dressing up as much more complex than they are, we want to try that against the solutions that we're piloting. We want to see if something we can do for, basically, free has almost the same value. We’ve considered hiring a couple developers to go deeper on this, but for now we’re just doing it with people we have in-house.

**Let’s dig into a few focus areas you mentioned are priorities for you. Starting with eligibility, what are you exploring there?** Eligibility denials are one of our top three denial categories, and it’s something we don’t control since we rely on hospital data feeds for patient demographics and coverage info. We’re now piloting both agentic AI and big data solutions that can double-check that information, either by validating it against payers directly or flagging discrepancies based on data from previous encounters or broader databases.

**What about denials management more broadly?** We’re already within the benchmark on overall denial rates, so our focus is getting ahead of denials. We’re piloting predictive models that flag claims likely to be denied before submission—whether it’s missing documentation, authorization gaps, or procedure mismatches. That gives our teams time to fix issues before the claim ever goes out the door.

**You’ve previously mentioned you’re exploring AI for the contact center, too—what does that entail?** We do about 50 million procedures a year, send out over 9 million patient statements, and take more than 100,000 calls each month, so we really had a need to optimize that for our patients. We’ve rolled out a product called Everyware for omni-channel patient communication. It handles text, email, and payment workflows. It helps us meet patients where they are, customize outreach, and improve collections.

On the call center side, we’re exploring agentic solutions, and separately deploying Birdseye, which monitors and responds to our practices’ Google reviews. Because of the volume of patients and procedures we see, even if we handle 99.5% of them correctly, 0.5% is still a very large number, and leads to negative reviews for our local practices. TMost negative reviews have nothing to do with clinical service, so this solution goes to those 99.5% of patients who have good experiences and encourages them to write a review. It also responds to every review that's written which helps your aggregate score.

**Of everything you’ve tried, where have you seen the biggest impact so far?** Two areas stand out: coding and patient experience. On the coding side, we’ve drastically reduced manual touches and dependency on an outsourced workforce. On the patient side, we’ve improved the experience and lifted collections by 15-20%. Those gains came from both new tech and basic blocking and tackling, like statement redesign and collection agency performance.
