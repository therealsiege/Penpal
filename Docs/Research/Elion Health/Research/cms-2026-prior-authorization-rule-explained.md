While providers are not directly regulated, they will experience operational impacts as payers adopt these systems. ## What Is the CMS 2026 Prior Authorization Rule?

The CMS 2026 Prior Authorization Rule sets a new national standard for [interoperability](https://elion.health/categories/interoperability) and administrative efficiency. It applies to payers offering Medicare Advantage (MA) plans, state Medicaid agencies, Children’s Health Insurance Program (CHIP) agencies, and payers with qualified health plans (QHPs) on the Affordable Care Act marketplaces.

A provider that primarily serves Medicare and Medicaid patients could have 30% to 60% or more of its total revenue connected to payers regulated under CMS-0057-F. This [impact is particularly significant](https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-in-2024-enrollment-update-and-key-trends/) in some markets with concentrated MA and managed Medicaid populations. Medicare Advantage plans now cover [nearly half of all beneficiaries nationwide](https://medicaidinnovation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Full_2024_MCO-Survey-FactSheetsReport-FINAL.pdf), and there are many geographies where most Medicaid beneficiaries receive their benefits  through managed care organizations.

The centerpiece of the rule is the requirement to use Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR)-based APIs to facilitate real-time data exchange among payers, providers, and patients. This shift reflects CMS’s continued investment in [interoperability infrastructure](https://www.cms.gov/health-technology-ecosystem/interoperability-framework) that supports automation, transparency, and patient empowerment. It’s the latest phase of a broader federal push to make healthcare data more connected, standardized, and actionable.

Key objectives include:

- **Streamlining the** [**prior authorization**](https://elion.health/categories/prior-authorization/products) **data exchange workflow** to both eliminate manual, paper-based workflows as well create a more cohesive, standardized process.

- **Improving transparency** by requiring detailed, standardized responses from payers with clear requirements and turnaround times.

- **Enabling providers to adopt solutions** that take advantage of new APIs, leading to reduced burden through automation and decision support systems.

- **Mandated turnaround times** that say standard requests must be completed in 7 days; expedited in 72 hours.

## Key Changes Under the Interoperability and Prior Auth Rule (CMS-0057-F)

The prior authorization changes coming in 2026 will reshape administrative workflows for both payers and providers, though the degree of real-world impact will depend heavily on execution. Healthcare leaders should be aware of the four key updates that will be implemented as part of the rule:

- **Accelerated Turnaround Timelines:** Payers must respond to standard requests [within 7 calendar days and expedited requests within 72 hours.](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-finalizes-rule-expand-access-health-information-and-improve-prior-authorization-process)

- **FHIR and ePA Integration Requirements:** All impacted payers must adopt FHIR APIs and ePA systems, enabling real-time interoperability between payer platforms and provider EHRs.

- **Denial Transparency:** Payers will be required to provide specific, structured denial reasons for claims.

- **Public Performance Reporting:** Payers must publish detailed metrics on prior authorization programs such as approval rates by procedure and overall decision times.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q.** **What are the CMS 2026 prior authorization requirements (CMS-0057-F)?**

A: CMS requires faster response times, use of FHIR APIs, electronic prior authorization (ePA), transparent denial communications, and public reporting from payers.

**Q.** **What is electronic prior authorization (ePA) and how does it work?**

A: ePA uses secure, standardized FHIR APIs to electronically transmit requests and responses between payers and providers. In other words, it replaces fax-based manual processes or payer portals.

**Q. What is the WISeR model for providers?**

A: The Workflow Integration & Smart Routing (WISeR) model integrates AI and FHIR-based automation to route and complete prior authorizations more efficiently. Starting on January 1, 2026, it will run for six performance years in New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Texas, Arizona, and Washington.

**Q.** **What should providers do now to prepare for the 2026 CMS Prior Auth Rule?**

A: Take steps to conduct readiness audits, modernize EHR systems, implement AI-enabled prior authorization solutions, and ensure staff are trained on compliance changes.

**Q. What’s the difference between FHIR prior authorization and traditional PA processes?**

A: FHIR prior authorization enables real-time, API-based data exchange, whereas traditional methods rely on manual submission, faxing, and delayed communication.
