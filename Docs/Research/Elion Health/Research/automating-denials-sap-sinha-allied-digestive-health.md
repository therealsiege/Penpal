# Automating Denials Outreach and Patient Recall with Sap Sinha of Allied Digestive Health

**Date:** July 11, 2025  
**Author:** Bobby Guelich, CEO, Elion

**Name:** Sap Sinha

**Role:** Chief Operating Officer

**Organization:** Allied Digestive Health

**Can you start with a quick overview of Allied Digestive Health and your role?** I’m the Chief Operating Officer at Allied Digestive Health, where I oversee all technology as part of my responsibilities. We operate 85 clinical offices across New York and New Jersey, making us the largest GI group in both states.

**What are your top technology priorities today?** Cybersecurity and tech optimization across our practices. With increasing threats, protecting our infrastructure is critical. At the same time, we’ve spent years piloting various tools, and now the focus is scaling what works across the organization and consolidating to a single platform wherever possible.

**Where are you using AI in clinical workflows today?** We use ambient documentation through [Suki](https://elion.health/products/suki), integrated with both EHRs we’re on, [athenahealth](https://elion.health/products/athenahealth) and [gGastro](https://elion.health/products/modmed). (Our practice is actually beta-testing gGastro’s branded version of Suki, G Scribe.) During colonoscopies, we use Medtronic’s GI Genius for real-time polyp detection. We’ve found that accuracy of that solution has improved significantly over the past four years, and it’s now widely accepted by our physicians. We also use [OpenEvidence](https://elion.health/products/openevidence), which offers clinical guidance similar to UpToDate but in a more conversational chat format. Beyond that, there are a handful of built-in AI tools within athenahealth that we’re experimenting with.

**Can you tell me more about the types of EHR-native AI tools you’ve been testing?** We’re most interested in tools to ease administrative burdens. Just like you might have 1,000 emails, clinicians have 1,000 clinical tasks, like handling prescription refills, patient messages, and order reviews. These are routine but time-consuming tasks, so we’ve been exploring athenahealth’s capabilities here.

**It seems like that’s a space where there have been a lot of point solutions with EMRs playing a bit of catch up. I’m curious about your opinion on how native solutions versus point solutions stack up.** It depends. For some areas like ambient AI or revenue cycle intelligence dashboards, point solutions are ahead. But for embedded workflow automation, like streamlining clinical tasks, it makes more sense for the EMR to own that. If EMRs don’t deliver, ambient or overlay solutions risk becoming the de facto user interface, and that dilutes the EMR’s value. So I think EMRs have a big incentive to close the gap.

**On the administrative side, where have you been investing in AI solutions?** We’ve invested in several areas. We started with a more traditional machine learning-based autonomous coding solution, but now are exploring more modern AI-based products. We use Adonis for RCM intelligence and we’re currently evaluating a new vendor to help us anticipate medical policy denials before a claim is submitted.

We’ve also deployed agents to contact payers, retrieve denial IDs, and push those back into athena. That task used to tie up staff for up to 30 to 40 minutes per call. Automating that has been a big relief. That said, we’re starting to hear that some payers may move to restrict the use of AI agents, so we’re watching that closely.

We’re also using agents for gaps-in-care outreach, like following up with patients due for a colonoscopy. What we realized is that these were very resource intensive jobs; maybe a recall was put in but it wasn’t followed through, or we did contact the patient but they didn’t call back. The AI can digest all the information in the medical record quickly, triage who needs outreach first, and then take the action to schedule that patient.

**You’d mentioned previously that patient payments were another area of focus. Any updates there?** We’re working with a company called [PayZen](https://elion.health/products/payzen), which does patient risk scoring and sets up non-recourse payment plans. They score patients and offer payment schedules with interest rates set based on an assessment of that patient’s ability to pay. We get 60-80% of the payment up front, and they handle the rest. It’s not a perfect solution, but it’s helped reduce financial burden on patients and get us paid more reliably.

**Any closing advice for peers?** Map the entire patient journey and think deeply about where AI can add value. And whatever solution you choose, it has to integrate with your core systems—especially the EMR and practice management. We’re not going to adopt another point solution unless it fully integrates with our workflow.
