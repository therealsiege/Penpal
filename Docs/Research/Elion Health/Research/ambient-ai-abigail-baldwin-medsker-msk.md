**Name:** [Abigail Baldwin Medsker](https://www.linkedin.com/in/abigailbaldwinmedsker/)

**Role:** Senior Director, Quality Optimization and Digital Health Innovation

**Organization:** [Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center](https://www.mskcc.org/)

**Can you give us a quick overview of your role?** I lead a team focused on quality optimization and digital health innovation projects. We focus on people, process, and technology, with a particular focus on AI at the moment, to tackle complex problems across the hospital. Sometimes that means helping us achieve broader strategic goals, and other times it’s about identifying opportunity spaces where we think we could add value in oncology care delivery.

**You recently** [**published**](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40902643/) **on your experience piloting an ambient AI scribe in oncology. What were the key takeaways?** This paper focused on our experience piloting this technology in 2024. Our goal was to assess feasibility, desirability, and acceptability of an ambient solution in oncology. Would oncologists use it? Did they find it effective? Could they see it helping with documentation burden and workflow in the future? The answer was yes.

Clinicians believed it would reduce burden, improve workflow, and even strengthen the patient-clinician experience. At the same time, they noted opportunities for the models to perform better in capturing oncology-specific details over time. The takeaway was it’s not perfect, but good enough, because there’s always a human in the loop and we believed the technology would get better over time.

That feedback became the foundation for business case development, and as of June 2025 we’ve been rolling out at full scale to any physician and APP. Now our evaluation is very keyed in on value delivery and outcomes like utilization, pajama time, documentation burden, ROI, as well as actually starting to measure the quality of the note in the oncology setting.

**What are you seeing so far at scale?** Utilization is high among those who choose to use it, though not everyone adopts it; it’s not a silver bullet. Some clinicians prefer other documentation support tools, some still like to write, and workflows vary. But for the clinicians who use it, they use it a lot, and anecdotally, they love it.

We’ll start digging into outcomes like pajama time and documentation burden in Q4 once we have access to the reporting feeds. Early signs are positive, but adoption curves take time.

**Is there anything unique about using this technology in oncology compared to primary care?** The core benefits—less documentation burden, more face time—are the same across specialties. What’s different is whether the model can reliably capture specialty-specific elements. Oncology visits are complex, and early on the tools weren’t always capturing everything. That’s improving over time.

One interesting thing we’re watching is that ambient notes can sometimes be longer and more robust than what clinicians are used to writing. That may be good, but it also means more to review and edit. We want to understand how that impacts workflow and satisfaction.

**What’s next beyond ambient documentation?** Two big areas:

- **Back-office efficiency:** We’re exploring AI for supply chain, CDI, HR workflows, and scheduling—anywhere we can achieve high-value ROI.

- **Clinical decision support:** We’re interested in tools that help reduce readmissions, shorten length of stay, and support end-of-life care in oncology. That means surfacing the right data at the right time to the right clinician coupled with workflow support.

Administrative AI is further along because the ROI is clearer and the risks lower. Clinical AI is earlier stage, but we think it will be critical once efficacy and workflow integration are proven.

**You’ve also helped stand up AI governance at MSK. Can you share more about your approach?** We’ve developed a governance lifecycle model, called [iLEAP](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01794-w#), based on Duke’s [ABCD](https://healthaigovernance.duke.edu/abcds-oversight) framework. Every high and medium risk project has to pass through oversight checkpoints, ensuring safety, transparency, and accountability.

Given that many hospitals are now standing up AI governance committees, for vendors, that means engaging not only with project sponsors but also with those committees, something many startups aren’t used to. For vendors that want to work with us, we expect transparency into model monitoring, clear documentation, and support the metrics. Vendors that come prepared with real case studies and evidence of value particularly stand out.

**How do you decide which AI projects to pursue?** We don’t believe in an “AI strategy” separate from the hospital strategy. MSK has strategic goals, and AI is one of the tools that can help us reach them. Sometimes opportunities are top-down, sometimes they come bottom-up from departments like radiology. Either way, with financial pressures across the industry, we’re focusing on high-value, high-impact business cases. We’re past the stage of piloting AI “just to see.” Every project now has to be justified by a strong business case with the exception of research and scientific studies.
