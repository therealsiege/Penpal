# Building Innovation Capacity in the Safety Net with Sofi Bergkvist of the Center for Care Innovations

**Date:** August 1, 2025  
**Author:** Bobby Guelich, CEO, Elion

---

**Name:** Sofi Bergkvist

**Role:** CEO and President

**Organization:** Center for Care Innovations

**Can you start by telling us about the Center for Care Innovations?** CCI is a nonprofit celebrating its 25th year, with a vision that everyone has fair, just, and inclusive opportunities to be healthy. Our mission is to spark, seed, and spread innovation across safety net healthcare providers, and, over the last couple of years, community based organizations and managed care plans.

We help providers define problems using human-centered design, often supporting frontline teams to build their own internal innovation capacity (“spark”); de-risk innovation (“seed”); and scale what works across our network (“spread”). For example, our Tech Hubs program in California has enabled peer sharing on innovation best practices for over a decade. We also support contract negotiations and evaluations, including ROI assessments, to ensure tech adoption is truly beneficial.

**What challenges are top of mind for your community today?** Workforce burnout and retention is a major concern across the sector, but especially acute in the safety net. Providers face the emotional toll of caring for patients with complex health and social needs in systems that don’t always provide them with enough support.

There’s also growing fear around layoffs, especially with Medicaid funding cuts. There's going to be a burning need for support with billing and compliance as well as making sure that our organizations are planning operations to have the cash flow that they need.

As one example, FQHCs have historically not billed for dual eligibles the way they could and should be. There are now organizations that are coming into FQHCs to take financial responsibility for these patients, and, by ensuring they’re coded properly, are driving positive financial results for both themselves and the FQHC.

Another urgent challenge is fear among immigrant communities around seeking care, which leads to delayed treatment and poorer outcomes.

**Have you seen any success stories around technology adoption that stand out?** AI tools to reduce no-show rates and improve scheduling have shown impact within weeks. We’ve also seen huge improvements from remote patient monitoring, especially for blood pressure control and diabetes management. Another successful implementation has been predictive modeling alongside SMS interventions to prevent ED visits among patients with asthma.

**What advice do you have for vendors who want to work with FQHCs or safety net orgs?** Vendors who understand Medicaid will be well-positioned in the coming years as we move toward value-based care. But recognize that entering this market takes time—educate yourself, be ready to adapt, and look for partners (like the California Health Care Foundation or Acumen Fund) that help de-risk this work through grants and investments.

There are also a number of technical needs specific to FQHCs that are often overlooked:

- Don’t underestimate language access—support for Spanish, Mandarin, and Vietnamese is critical.

- These organizations often use non-Epic EHRs. So be prepared to work with a broader range of EHR vendors.

- Offline functionality is often overlooked, both for providers and patients who might not have reliable internet.

More than anything, understand the problems. For example, we need more solutions that address social determinants—like food insecurity or housing instability—in ways that are tightly linked to clinical care.

It feels like there's a solution for everything right now, so whatever I say we need, someone will come and say, “No, but that exists.” Does it exist at a price point that works? Does it exist in a way that’s accessible and can be integrated?

**Any final thoughts?** We’re in a time of real pressure, but also opportunity. The system we have isn’t working, and I hope these stressors push us to collaborate across sectors and do things we haven’t done before. The safety net needs innovation and is ready for it. Vendors who show up, listen, and build with these communities will make a lasting difference.
