### 1. H&L Deep Dive

Today we’re reviewing a patient chart using eSpiral, focusing on a visit from **January 14, 2024**, where the chief complaint was **cough**.

Using the left-hand panel, we’ll curate the problem list from approximately **15 entries** down to just those **relevant to the cough**.

1. **Select**:
    - *Chronic systolic congestive heart failure*
2. **Suppress**:
    - *Atherosclerosis of aorta, unspecified*
    - *Other specified peripheral vascular disease*
    
    These entries often include **copy/paste duplicates**.
    
    By clicking the **filter icon**, we can **collapse these duplicates** down to a single entry, simplifying the view.
    
3. **Continue curating**:
    - **Include**:
        - *Malignant neoplasm of ascending colon*
        - *Malignant neoplasm of upper lobe of right lung*
            
            (which could potentially be **metastatic** from the colon cancer)
            
4. **Suppress**:
    - *Urinary incontinence*

Now we’re entering the **2022 time frame**. Again, we use the **filter** to collapse copy/paste duplicates.

We also find that *malignant neoplasm of right lung* appears in a previous orbit from **August 22, 2021**.

1. **Suppress**:
    - *Motion sickness*
    - *Hypercholesterolemia*
    - *Unrelated entry from March 17, 2009*
2. **Include**:
    - *Pneumonia, organism unspecified*
        
        (likely relevant to the cough)
        
    - *Tobacco use disorder*
        
        (important for the **etiology of lung cancer**)
        
    - *Gastro-esophageal reflux disease* from **July 6, 2006**
        
        (possible contributor to the cough)
        

At this point, we also notice **collision problems**—issues that may relate across multiple time frames.

We can **reveal these** with the appropriate button, and the **text box icon** provides a description correlating with the entries in the left panel.

Using eSpiral, we’ve just **interrogated nearly a decade of clinical history**, going **far deeper** than the typical resident review, which often focuses only on **recent events prior to an ED visit or admission**.

This demonstrates the **power of eSpiral** in enabling comprehensive, high-fidelity chart reviews that surface **meaningful connections** from across the longitudinal patient record.
