# 1Putt Health — Competitive Positioning

Last updated: 2026-03-14

---

## Positioning Statement

1Putt Health is the only boutique healthcare technology consultancy that combines deep FHIR/Epic integration expertise with HIPAA compliance consulting, purpose-built for Series A-C digital health startups.

We don't serve health systems. We don't sell platforms. We help the companies building the next generation of healthcare technology get their architecture, integrations, and compliance right — fast.

---

## Why 1Putt Wins

Three things set 1Putt apart from every competitor in this market:

**1. We build, not just advise.** MedScrub (clinical AI for independent practices) and MedHook (healthcare data integration platform, formerly Retrohook) are active products maintained by the same team that serves consulting clients. When we recommend an architecture, it's because we've shipped it. When we flag a FHIR implementation risk, it's because we've hit it in our own code. Consultancies that only advise can't match this.

**2. Full stack, not single capability.** Most healthcare consultancies do integrations OR compliance OR strategy. 1Putt covers the full surface area a digital health startup needs: FHIR integration, Epic connectivity, HIPAA/HITRUST readiness, architecture design, and technical leadership. One vendor, one relationship, one team that understands the whole picture.

**3. Startup speed, enterprise depth.** We operate at startup velocity — 48-hour proposal turnaround, week-one productivity, no SOW committees. But our expertise comes from enterprise healthcare: Epic, Optum, large-scale clinical data systems. Clients get big-company knowledge delivered at startup pace.

## Competitive Landscape

### Direct Competitors

#### vs Osplabs
**Who they are:** 15-30 person healthcare integration consultancy. Strong SEO presence for "Epic FHIR consulting." Active blog and content marketing. Primarily focused on Epic integrations.

**Where they're strong:**
- Dominant search presence for Epic/FHIR consulting keywords
- Established content engine (blog posts, case studies)
- Team depth — can staff larger projects

**Where 1Putt wins:**
- Osplabs is Epic-focused; 1Putt covers the full EHR landscape (Epic, Oracle Health, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks) plus compliance
- No compliance practice — they can build the integration but can't help you pass the security review
- Larger team means higher overhead and less senior attention per client
- They advise on integrations; we build and maintain integration products (MedHook)

**How to position against them in a deal:**
"Osplabs does solid Epic integration work. If your only need is Epic connectivity, they're worth evaluating. But if you also need HIPAA readiness, architecture beyond Epic, or senior technical leadership embedded in your team, they'll refer you out for those — and you'll end up managing two or three vendors. We cover the full surface area."

---

#### vs Itirra
**Who they are:** 10-20 person healthcare IT consultancy, established 10+ years. Generalist health IT consulting including EHR implementations, system migrations, and project management.

**Where they're strong:**
- Long track record — 10+ years in business builds trust with risk-averse buyers
- Broader health IT scope (EHR implementations, system migrations, PM)
- Established client base and referral network

**Where 1Putt wins:**
- Itirra is a generalist health IT shop; 1Putt is a specialist in integration + compliance for digital health startups
- Weak or nonexistent HIPAA/HITRUST compliance practice
- Their clientele skews toward health systems and established health IT companies, not venture-backed startups
- Less depth in modern FHIR architecture — stronger in legacy EHR implementation and migration

**How to position against them in a deal:**
"Itirra has been around a long time and they know health IT broadly. If you need help migrating an EHR or managing a health system IT project, they're solid. For a venture-backed company building modern FHIR-native integrations and needing to get compliance-ready for enterprise sales, our focus is more relevant."

### Enterprise Competitors (Not Direct, But Come Up in Conversation)

#### vs Nordic / Tegria / Optimum Healthcare IT
**Who they are:** Large healthcare IT consulting firms. Nordic has 1,000+ consultants. Tegria (now part of KLAS Research ecosystem) similarly large. Optimum focuses on Epic consulting.

**Where they serve:** $500M+ health systems, large payer organizations, academic medical centers. Enterprise procurement, long sales cycles, large team deployments.

**Why they're not direct competitors:**
- Minimum engagement size is typically $200K+
- Sales cycles run 3-6 months through procurement
- They staff projects with consultants, not with principals
- Their clients are health systems, not the startups selling to health systems

**When they come up:**
A prospect who has worked at a health system may reference Nordic or Tegria. The response: "Those firms serve health systems with large IT departments. You're a 30-person startup that needs a senior technical partner, not a team of 10 consultants managed by a project manager. We're a different animal — smaller, faster, and built for companies at your stage."

### Platform Providers (Complementary, Not Competitive)

#### vs Redox
**What they are:** Integration-platform-as-a-service. Provides a unified API layer over EHR systems. Handles transport (connectivity to health systems) so developers don't have to manage VPN tunnels, HL7v2 feeds, and per-site configuration.

**Why they're not competitive:** Redox is infrastructure. 1Putt is expertise. A startup might use Redox AND hire 1Putt. Redox handles the pipe; 1Putt designs what flows through it, how the data maps to the application, and how the architecture passes a security review.

**When they come up:**
"Should we use Redox?" is a common prospect question. The answer: "Maybe. It depends on your integration volume, your target EHR mix, and your build-vs-buy calculus. Redox charges per-connection fees that get expensive at scale. For 1-3 health system connections, direct FHIR may be simpler and cheaper. For 10+, Redox starts to make economic sense. This is exactly the kind of decision we help clients make — and if you go with Redox, we handle the application-layer integration that Redox doesn't cover."

#### vs Smile CDR / HAPI FHIR
**What they are:** Smile CDR is a commercial FHIR server platform. HAPI FHIR is its open-source foundation. Companies use them to build FHIR-native data infrastructure.

**When they come up:**
Companies evaluating whether to build their own FHIR server (HAPI), buy one (Smile CDR), or use a SaaS integration layer (Redox). 1Putt helps make this decision and implements whichever path is chosen.

#### vs Health Gorilla
**What they are:** Clinical data network providing access to lab results, medications, clinical documents across provider networks. API-first.

**When they come up:**
Companies needing clinical data access without direct EHR integration. Health Gorilla covers a specific use case (data retrieval from the Commonwell/Carequality networks). 1Putt helps evaluate whether Health Gorilla meets the client's data needs or whether direct EHR integration is required.

### The "We'll Hire Internally" Competitor

This is the most common competitive loss — the prospect decides to hire a full-time VP Engineering or integration team instead of engaging 1Putt.

**How to position:**
- Speed: 1Putt is productive in week 1. A VP Eng hire takes 4-6 months from job posting to productive contribution.
- Risk: 1Putt has done this before. A new hire is learning on the job.
- Cost: Fractional CTO at $12.5K/month = $150K/year. Full-time VP Eng = $280-350K salary + $50-100K in equity, benefits, recruiting. 1Putt is 35-45% of the cost.
- Flexibility: Retainer can scale up or down. Employee is a fixed cost with termination complexity.
- Complementary: 1Putt can serve as fractional CTO while the client recruits full-time, then transition to advisory or project-based work. Not either/or.

4. **"We're based in Nashville."** Nashville is the healthcare capital of the US — HCA, Vanderbilt Health, Change Healthcare (Optum), Community Health Systems, Envision. We're embedded in the ecosystem, not flying in from the coasts.

This matters for companies building clinical AI, population health analytics, or intelligent data platforms.

## Messaging Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Lead with the client's problem, not 1Putt's capabilities
- Use specific numbers (timelines, costs, team sizes) rather than vague claims
- Reference the products (MedScrub, MedHook) as proof of builder credibility
- Acknowledge competitor strengths before explaining differentiation
- Frame 1Putt as a partner, not a vendor

**Don't:**
- Trash competitors by name in writing (in conversation, be direct but professional)
- Claim to be "full-service" — we're specialists, and that's the point
- Compare on hourly rate — we lose that comparison against offshore shops and win it against enterprise firms, neither of which is useful
- Oversell team size — we're a boutique firm and that's a feature, not a bug
- Promise capabilities we don't have (e.g., large team staffing, 24/7 support, regulatory legal advice)
