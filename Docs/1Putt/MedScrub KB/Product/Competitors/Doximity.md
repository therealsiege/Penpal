## Company Overview

Doximity is the largest professional medical network in the US with 80% of doctors, NPs, and PAs registered. Publicly traded (NASDAQ: DOCS), generating ~$185M/quarter. Adding AI features (DoxGPT, Scribe) as product expansions, not standalone products.

### Key Facts

- Founded: 2010 | HQ: San Francisco | CEO: Jeff Tangney | Public: NASDAQ: DOCS
- Q3 FY2026: Revenue $185.1M (10% YoY), Net income $61.6M (33%), EBITDA $111.4M (60%)
- 3M+ verified US clinicians on platform (80% of US docs)
- Revenue model: Pharma marketing, hiring solutions, telehealth, AI features

## AI Features

- DoxGPT — HIPAA-compliant AI assistant for clinical questions, writing, drug references
- PeerCheck™ — physician-verified, evidence-based AI answers (unique differentiator)
- Scribe — ambient AI scribe for SOAP notes (in-app + integrated with Dialer telehealth)
- Dialer — HIPAA-compliant voice/video with caller ID masking
- Digital Fax, Newsfeed, Pathway clinical decision support
- All AI features FREE for verified US clinicians

## Strengths & Weaknesses

**Strengths:** MASSIVE distribution (80% of US docs), free AI features, profitable (60%+ EBITDA), trusted brand, integrated workflow (call → scribe → note), no audio storage, PeerCheck evidence layer.

**Weaknesses:** AI is feature add-on, not core (revenue from pharma ads). Scribe is mobile-first, no deep EHR integration, no CDR, no prior auth, no coding/billing. May not invest deeply in AI if not core revenue.

## MedScrub Differentiation

Doximity is the elephant in the room — 80% of US docs already use it. Their free Scribe could become 'good enough' for basic notes. But Doximity makes money from pharma ads, not clinical tools. AI will remain a feature, not a platform.

MedScrub goes deeper: PHI proxy (real data safety), CDR (persistent patient data), prior auth (revenue protection). Doximity won't build these — they don't need to.

🚨 Biggest distribution threat. Monitor Scribe evolution. If they add EHR integrations or coding, reassess immediately.
