## Executive Summary

Provider Data Management (PDM) has become a strategic priority for healthcare organizations facing growing regulatory, financial, and operational pressures. Fragmented provider data across EHRs, HR, and credentialing systems leads to costly inaccuracies—contributing to $17 billion in annual claim denials. The No Surprises Act and CMS rules now demand continuous verification and transparency. Vendors address PDM through master data platforms, credentialing workflows, and patient access tools. As interoperability and AI-driven governance advance, PDM is evolving from a compliance obligation into a foundational capability for improving patient access, revenue integrity, and enterprise data accuracy

The U.S. healthcare industry [spends](https://virsys12.com/the-2-76-billion-provider-network-problem-healthcare-cant-ignore/) $2.76 billion annually on provider directory maintenance, yet accuracy problems persist. A 2023 Senate Finance Committee [study](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/050323%20Ghost%20Network%20Hearing%20-%20Secret%20Shopper%20Study%20Report.pdf) revealed that 33% of provider directory listings contain inaccurate information or non-working numbers with only 18% of contact attempts resulting in appointments. These “ghost networks” drive patient confusion and dissatisfaction as they seek care.

The core problem is that the provider data needed to power directories and other primary functions is both fragmented and dynamic, and lives in disconnected silos. In practice, this means the data may be spread out across HR systems, [Electronic Health Records (EHRs)](https://elion.health/categories/electronic-health-record-ehr/products), credentialing systems, contracting systems, and various other locations across the enterprise.

**Provider Data Management (PDM) is the enterprise process and system for unifying this data and creating a definitive record for every provider, organization, and location.** PDM ingests, matches, and governs data to build a single, authoritative source of truth. A robust PDM strategy helps ensure patient safety, protect revenue cycle integrity, and meet ever-stricter regulatory demands.

## The Current State of Provider Data Management: A Perfect Storm of Market Pressures

The provider data management market is maturing rapidly, driven by a convergence of regulatory and financial forces.

The federal No Surprises Act [mandates](https://www.mdclarity.com/blog/no-surprises-act-provider-directory) that health plans verify provider directories at least every 90 days and update information within two business days of receiving changes. Similarly, CMS now [requires](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/19/2025-18236/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-contract-year-2026-policy-and-technical-changes-to-the-medicare) Medicare Advantage plans to update their directories within 30 days of any change and attest annually to accuracy. While these rules apply to payers, they’ve pushed providers to prioritize data accuracy as well, since the same systems feed both internal directories and payer networks, and the reputational impact of inaccurate information ultimately falls on the providers themselves.

The financial incentives are equally strong. Inaccurate provider information is a leading cause of claim denials, [contributing](https://www.madakethealth.com/blogs/the-downstream-effects-of-poor-provider-data-management-why-we-need-to-improve-provider-directories) nearly $17 billion annually in unnecessary healthcare costs. This risk intensifies during mergers and acquisitions, when combining provider rosters, contracts, and billing systems often exposes conflicting records and outdated data, leading to significant revenue leakage. For many organizations, the cost of inaction now outweighs the cost of fixing provider data, accelerating investment in PDM solutions.

## Vendor Landscape

The PDM market can be grouped into three primary clusters:

**1\. Enterprise Master Data Management Platforms**

These are platforms that treat PDM as a classic, cross-domain, master-data challenge. Their primary focus is on creating a technically pure [golden record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_record_(informatics)) for each provider through advanced identity resolution and governance across all enterprise systems. These platforms address the core IT problem of inconsistent and duplicate provider data scattered across numerous enterprise systems. Notable vendors in this space include [Verato](https://elion.health/products/verato) and [Gaine](https://elion.health/products/gaine-provider-data-management).

**2\. Credentialing & Enrollment Solutions**

These solutions embed PDM capabilities directly into high-pain operational workflows, such as [provider credentialing, privileging, and payer enrollment](https://elion.health/categories/credentialing-licensing-and-enrollment/products). Data is mastered as a byproduct of essential administrative processes. They address the significant administrative burden of onboarding new providers and ensuring they can bill for services compliantly and without delay. Notable vendors that have evolved from a workflow-centric heritage include [symplr Provider](https://elion.health/products/symplr-provider), [Medallion](https://elion.health/products/medallion), [CertifyOS](https://elion.health/products/certifyos), and [Madaket](https://elion.health/products/madaket).

**3\. Patient Access & Directory Platforms**

These platforms are oriented around the downstream use of provider data to power patient-facing "find-a-doctor" tools, online scheduling, and digital marketing initiatives. They master data with the primary goal of making it discoverable and actionable for consumers. These solutions address the strategic goal of improving patient access, reducing patient leakage, and managing the organization's "digital front door." Notable vendors leading this engagement-focused category include [Kyruus](https://elion.health/products/kyruus) and [Loyal](https://elion.health/products/loyal).

## Future Outlook

Two forces will reshape PDM over the next few years: continued technological progress through AI and national interoperability infrastructure.

Technology now directly addresses many historical PDM pain points. Platforms apply ML for identity resolution and record linkage, to dedupe conflicting profiles across HR/EHR/credentialing/contracting systems, and to flag anomalies as data changes. Generative tools summarize credentialing packets, reconcile licensing/privileging, and draft provider outreach. Coupled with rules engines, this reduces manual stewardship and increases auditability.

Additionally, the [proposed](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-asks-public-input-establishing-first-national-directory-health-care-providers-and-services) National Directory of Healthcare Providers & Services (NDH), delivered via [FHIR‑based APIs](https://hl7.org/fhir/us/ndh/2023Sep/), could accelerate data exchange for provider data. Timelines and details remain fluid, however. Even if implemented soon, PDM will be required to support NDH requirements. Health systems will still need to master internal data, map local identifiers to standard profiles, orchestrate bidirectional flows between NDH endpoints and enterprise systems, and enrich NDH data with operational context (contracts, schedules, locations, services) for patient access, revenue cycle, and compliance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### **Q. What is Provider Data Management (PDM)?**

**A.** Provider Data Management (PDM) is the process of collecting, validating, and governing provider information—such as credentials, affiliations, and locations—across healthcare systems. It ensures that data is accurate, consistent, and accessible across HR, EHR, and contracting platforms.

### **Q. Why is accurate provider data important?**

**A:** Accurate provider data prevents claim denials, improves patient access, and helps healthcare organizations stay compliant with CMS and No Surprises Act requirements. Inaccuracies can lead to millions in revenue loss and regulatory penalties.

### **Q. What causes provider data inaccuracy?**

**A:** Data fragmentation across multiple systems—EHRs, credentialing platforms, and HR databases—creates silos that make it difficult to maintain a single source of truth. Frequent provider movement and organizational changes further compound the challenge.

### **Q. How does the No Surprises Act impact provider data management?**

**A:** The No Surprises Act mandates that health plans verify provider directories every 90 days and update data within two business days of any change. This regulation drives health systems to adopt more robust PDM tools and workflows to stay compliant.

### **Q. What are the key benefits of a Provider Data Management system?**

**A:** A strong PDM system improves data accuracy, reduces administrative burden, enhances patient experience, and safeguards revenue by minimizing claim rejections and credentialing delays.
