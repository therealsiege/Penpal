U.S. hospitals [wrote off](https://essentialhospitals.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/CBO-Analysis-Reconciliation-June-2025.pdf) roughly $41 billion in uncompensated care last year while facing mounting financial pressures from denied claims and inefficient utilization. According to recent industry analysis, hospitals are [spending](https://premierinc.com/newsroom/policy/claims-adjudication-costs-providers-257-billion-18-billion-is-potentially-unnecessary-expense) an estimated $25.7 billion annually appealing denials, while [approximately](https://www.advisory.com/content/dam/advisory/en/public/shared/Research/PACC/Resources/2019/Data-Primer-Avoidable-Hospital-Days.pdf) 25% of hospital days (nearly 10.8 million days) are potentially avoidable, equivalent to 29,600 full hospital beds occupied for a year.

Against that backdrop, two intertwined functions do most of the heavy lifting: [utilization review (UR) and case management (CM)](https://elion.health/categories/provider-utilization-review/products). Utilization review (also frequently referred to as utilization management, especially on the payer-side) focuses on ensuring medical necessity and appropriate levels of care, while case management coordinates the patient's care progression and discharge planning. Together, they form a continuous workflow that spans the entire patient journey from admission decisions through discharge planning and transition management.

# **Workflow Overview**

The UR/CM workflow typically involves multiple roles  working in concert:

- UR nurses review clinical documentation against evidence-based criteria to validate medical necessity.

- Case managers coordinate care plans and arrange post-acute services.

- Physician advisors help resolve complex status determinations and payer disputes.

- Social workers address non-clinical barriers to discharge.

- Payer representatives must authorize continued stays and services.

The relationship between providers and payers is particularly crucial in UR/CM. While historically adversarial, with payers deploying aggressive denial strategies and providers struggling to appeal them, modern UR/CM solutions aim to foster collaboration through shared data platforms and standardized criteria. This matters because when providers and payers align on medical necessity determinations upfront, it reduces denials, speeds up authorizations, and ultimately improves patient care transitions.

Today's solutions typically integrate several key capabilities:

- Automated medical necessity screening using evidence-based criteria (like InterQual or MCG)

- AI-powered predictive analytics for status determination and length-of-stay optimization

- Workflow automation for case prioritization and documentation

- Real-time payer connectivity for streamlined authorizations

- Integrated discharge planning and post-acute coordination

# **Market Landscape**

The vendor landscape broadly breaks down into:

**Guideline Criteria Engines**: [MCG Health Indicia](https://elion.health/products/mcg-health) and [InterQual from Optum](https://elion.health/products/interqual-autoreview) provide the evidence-based criteria that power most UR decisions. These platforms increasingly offer direct EHR integration and automated review capabilities.

**AI-Powered UR Optimization**: [XSOLIS Dragonfly Utilize](https://elion.health/products/xsolis-utilize) and [Iodine AwareUM](https://elion.health/products/iodine-aware-um) h leverage machine learning to prioritize cases, predict status determinations, and streamline reviews. Both platforms report significant reductions in inappropriate status assignments and manual work.

**Physician Workflow Support**: Solutions like [EvidenceCare's AdmissionCare](https://elion.health/products/evidencecare-admissioncare) focus on guiding physicians at the point of admission to ensure correct initial status determinations.

**Post-Acute Coordination**: [WellSky CarePort](https://elion.health/products/wellsky-careport) and [naviHealth](https://elion.health/products/navihealth) specialize in discharge planning and post-acute placement, connecting hospitals to networks of post-acute providers for electronic referrals.

Looking ahead, the category is being shaped by several key trends. CMS's mandate for electronic prior authorization via FHIR APIs by 2026-2027 will accelerate payer-provider integration. Meanwhile, AI and automation capabilities continue advancing, with many platforms now offering automated appeal generation and real-time status predictions. As denial rates remain high and staffing challenges persist, expect continued investment in solutions that can demonstrate measurable impact on medical necessity denials, length of stay, and operational efficiency.
