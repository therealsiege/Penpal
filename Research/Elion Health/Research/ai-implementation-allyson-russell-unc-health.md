**Date:** September 12, 2025  
**Author:** Bobby Guelich, CEO, Elion

**Name:** Allyson Russell

**Role:** Leader, Intelligent Optimization

**Organization:** [UNC Health](https://www.unchealth.org/home)

**Can you start with an overview of your role?**

I focus on how AI and automation can positively impact our healthcare system and improve care for our patients. Specifically, my role is to work closely with the business leaders to identify their strategic priorities, understand their opportunities and pain points, optimize or re-engineer the workflow, and then figure out a way we can apply technology to optimize those workflows. It’s really no different than any other technology problem, but now we have this AI and automation capability that we didn't have before, which enables us to tackle more complex issues at a faster pace.

**What does that look like in practice?**

My approach is to meet with executives, sometimes with one of their supervisors or subject matter experts and run structured brainstorming sessions. We stack-rank priorities based on their strategic fit and metrics. From there, I dig deep into the process: How does it work? How often does it happen? How long does it take? What happens if it doesn’t get done? That detective work helps us decide the right tool for the job.

Sometimes the solution is a simple integration, application enhancement, or SQL. Sometimes it’s robotic process automation or an AI agent. The key is matching the right tool to the business problem.

**Once you’ve identified the problem, how do you decide whether to build, buy, or partner?**

First, I triage: Is this worth doing, and what kind of value would it bring? Then we ask: Can the applications we already use solve this? Could our team configure one of those applications differently to address the challenge? If not, is it on one of the vendor’s roadmaps—and if so, is the timeline acceptable? Otherwise, it may make sense to automate and integrate ourselves. We may also consider bridge solutions that temporarily address a need, fully realizing that it could result in short-term solutions that will be thrown away.

**You mentioned value. How do you define and measure that?**

For us, value starts with people. Our priority is improving patient care, saving lives, and reducing burden on our teammates—physicians, APPs, nurses, and staff. That’s the impact we want to celebrate and highlight when we talk about success. Early build projects may not be the ones with the biggest dollar ROI; they’re the ones that demonstrate this success, reduce burden, and help people appreciate what the technology can do. Over time, that has built trust so teammates themselves can start bringing the bigger, more impactful, higher ROI ideas forward.  Augmenting teammates to perform higher functioning cognitive tasks allows everyone to perform at the top of their skill level, while also promoting efficiency, financial benefits, clinical and administrative quality, etc.

**Do you have an example of a project you’ve implemented that has had a significant impact across patients’ or teammates’ lives?**

One significantly impactful project is an example of collaborating with an existing vendor partner to achieve a needed outcome. Epic’s infusion scheduling dynamically allocates chairs for cancer patients, reducing wait times and balancing nurse schedules. That’s a clear quality-of-life improvement for both patients and staff, but it also increased chair utilization—so the efficiency and ROI is there, too.

**Are there any hard ROI-focused project examples you can share?**

Eligibility denial automation is a good example. We had tens of thousands of denials requiring manual touch each month. Staffing to handle them wasn’t feasible, leading to write-offs. By automating, we reduce timely filing denials and measure avoided write-offs directly. That’s a hard-dollar win. We were able to prove ROI by building measurements into the project from the start. The key lesson is to define how to track savings or impact before you implement.

**Epic just announced a wave of new AI capabilities. How do you think about platform features versus third-party solutions?**

I was excited by Epic’s roadmap, especially around agentic AI. Anytime we can partner with Epic to optimize a situation, we will evaluate those capabilities. If Epic can meet the need, we’ll implement the solution. But we’re not closed off to third-party and homegrown platform and point solutions. Our goal is to balance transformational progress with the best benefits for our people and organization.
