For years, patients and providers have lamented the painful reality of prior authorization (PA). There’s change in the air though. If the two announcements from AHIP and CMS last month are any indication, the old status quo may be going away in favor of a digitized, AI-assisted, FHIR-based future.

First, via [AHIP](https://www.ahip.org/), major insurers [issued](https://www.ahip.org/news/press-releases/health-plans-take-action-to-simplify-prior-authorization) a joint pledge promising to slash prior authorization friction through six key commitments including both standardizing FHIR-based submissions and delivering real-time decisions for 80% of electronic requests by 2027. These changes don’t arrive in a vacuum. In January 2024, CMS [announced](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-finalizes-rule-expand-access-health-information-and-improve-prior-authorization-process) that certain payers needed to adopt mandatory PA APIs and significantly shortened PA decision timelines by 2026. The commitments in the pledge all mirror the new CMS rules allowing the industry to present a united, “we’re fixing it” front to Congress and regulators.

Next, just a week after the AHIP announcement, CMS's [Innovation Center](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/overview) announced [WISeR](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/wiser), a new pilot bringing AI-enabled prior authorization to traditional Medicare. Starting January 2026, this six-year program will deploy AI and machine learning tools to streamline authorizations for certain services particularly vulnerable to fraud and overuse.

On their face, the AHIP and WISeR announcements might seem contradictory: commercial insurers vowing to reduce prior authorizations and CMS starting to utilize them in new areas. However, both moves point to the same end state: prior authorization becomes an AI-guided, real-time, data exchange utilized primarily for targeted use-cases where fraud or overuse is proven, as opposed to the blunt instrument it’s often been.

# **The Prior Authorization Crisis**

While prior authorization was initially designed to ensure appropriate, evidence-based care, the administrative burden has become overwhelming for healthcare providers.

According to a 2024 AMA [survey](https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/prior-authorization-survey.pdf), physicians and their staff currently spend an average of 13 hours per week processing approximately 39 prior authorization requests, with 40% of physicians employing staff who work exclusively on PA. Beyond the administrative overhead, there’s a serious patient impact: 29% of physicians report that PA has led to a serious adverse event for a patient in their care, and 60% report patients often abandoning their prescribed treatments due to authorization obstacles.

# **Market Landscape for AI-Enabled Prior Authorization**

While a number of solutions serve multiple segments, [AI PA solutions](https://elion.health/categories/ai-prior-authorization-for-providers/products) can generally be subcategorized by the segment of the market served:

- **Medications:** Platforms focused on authorizing therapies with an emphasis on high-cost specialty drugs. Some examples include [SamaCare](https://elion.health/products/samacare), [Develop Health](https://elion.health/products/develop-health), and [Tandem](https://elion.health/products/tandem).

- **Medical Services:** Solutions tailored to hospital and health system authorizations for a wide-range of non-drug services including surgical procedures, advanced imaging, and acute care. Examples in this category include [AKASA](https://elion.health/products/akasa-authorization-advisor) and [Notable](http://elion.health/products/notable-authorizations).

- **Specialized Segments:** Niche solutions for specific care areas such as [Silna](https://elion.health/products/silna), which focuses on physical therapy, occupational therapy, and a number of other specialities, or [Concert](https://elion.health/products/concert) for genetic testing PAs.

These AI-powered solutions combine natural language processing to read clinical documentation, machine learning to interpret complex payer policies, and robotic process automation to navigate portals and forms. Applying AI assistance or automation massively cuts the hours of staff time reviewing charts, logging into systems, and following up with payers.

Core capabilities include:

- Checking whether services require prior authorization upon ordering

- Pulling relevant patient information from medical records and clinical notes

- Matching patient data against each payer's specific coverage criteria

- Auto-filling authorization request forms with required clinical details

- Submitting requests through various channels (payer portals, APIs, fax)

- Tracking authorization status and alerting staff when action is needed

- Generating appeal letters if requests are denied

In addition to strictly PA-focused solutions, there are a number of complementary AI solutions that enable automation of key tasks without allowing full end-to-end PA. [Infinitus Systems](https://elion.health/products/infinitus) or [Superdial](https://elion.health/products/superdial) utilize AI voice agents for payer calls that are part of the PA process. [Tennr](https://elion.health/products/tennr) offers document processing and general workflow automation applicable to PA.

# **The Future of Prior Authorization**

Picture a clinician chatting naturally with a patient while ambient AI instantly translates their conversation into structured clinical notes. In parallel, the EHR proactively identifies the need for PA, automatically compiles relevant clinical data, and submits an electronic PA request—all seamlessly before the clinician signs off. The building blocks to orchestrate this vision are already taking shape through ambient documentation, ambient ordering, and AI-driven autofill of PA forms directly from chart data, all underpinned by robust interoperability standards with [APIs](https://build.fhir.org/ig/HL7/davinci-pas/usecases.html) that aim for real-time authorization decisions at the point of care.

While the convergence of regulatory mandates, payer commitments, and Medicare's AI pilot are all aligning to drive significant change in the PA landscape, the more interesting long-term transformations may come from the recent rapid changes in technology. Automation via AI agents, ambient scribing kicking off workflows in the background, and fully interoperable, real-time integrations will all allow the PA process to evolve from a burdensome, manual task into a nearly invisible, automated background service.
